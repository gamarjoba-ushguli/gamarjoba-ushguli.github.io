Rooms

The rooms are upstairs. Two of them are doubles, and two have three beds. Each room has its own bathroom.

Some of the rooms have a balcony, and the mountains are on the other side of the window.

The table

The dining room is on the ground floor. You eat at a table, on wooden chairs of the kind the village elder traditionally used, and on a bench that Fridon Nizharadze carved. The food is home-cooked and regional.

You do not have to be staying the night. If you are walking past on the way to the glacier or back from it, you can ask for a meal.

The paintings of Fridon Nizharadze

There is a small gallery in the house. The paintings in it are by Fridon Nizharadze, and Fridon is one of the family.

They are not a collection that was bought and hung. They belong to the house, the way the bench in the dining room does — he carved that too, and you have already sat on it.

Every house in Ushguli looks out at the same mountains.
This is the one with Fridon’s paintings inside it.

Where it is

Ushguli is a group of villages at the head of the Enguri valley in Svaneti, at about 2,100 metres, and its stone towers are a UNESCO World Heritage site. This house stands at the upper edge of it, close to St George church and Lamaria church.

Fifty metres away there is a hill, and it is the highest point of the village. From the top you see the whole of Ushguli one way and Shkhara, the highest peak in Georgia, the other. The trail to the Shkhara glacier starts here. So does the last day of the walk from Mestia, in reverse.

There is an ethnographic museum near the house, in an old Svanetian house — a machubi.
